# Practice Talk Track

**Figure:** `img/natural_size_violin.png` — Natural-cause fire-size distribution by year
**Target:** ~90 seconds

---

## Describe

Every natural-causes wildfire in the country — lightning, essentially — 1992 to 2020. About 327,000 fires, one violin per year. Acres on a log axis. The red bar is the median.

Two things. In any single year, the typical fire is a tenth of an acre and the largest is half a million. And nothing about the shape of those violins really changes across thirty years — the biggest fire in 1994 is about the biggest fire in 2020.

The gold line is the trend in total acres burned per year, on the right axis — a straight line fit through the annual totals, not the totals themselves. That trend roughly doubles, from about two and a half million acres to about five. Same-looking fires, twice the area.

## Interpret

So counting fires and measuring acres answer different questions. Most fires burn very little; a handful burn nearly everything. Rank by count and you're ranking on the fires that don't matter.

And the pattern isn't holding still. Whatever is driving that gold line, a model fit to the 1990s is describing a smaller fire year than the one a planner is about to face.

## Implication

For a planner deciding where limited prevention money goes: I rank causes by expected acres burned, not by how often they ignite. And I train on the past and test on the future, because the recent years are the ones that describe today.

That's the design this figure forced.
